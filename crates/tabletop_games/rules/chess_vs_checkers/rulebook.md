# Chess vs Checkers

The same board, one army each from two different games: what happens when the
pieces of checkers meet the pieces of chess.

## Players

Two: Chess and Checkers. The first player to move commands the chess army; the
first other player to move after them commands the checkers army.

## The pieces

Chess sets up its whole army as in chess, on its own side. Checkers sets up
twelve men on the dark squares of the three rows on the other side.

## A turn

Chess moves first, then the players take turns. Every piece moves the way it
does in its own game, and captures whatever enemy piece it would capture there:

- A chess piece captures a man or a king of checkers by landing on it.
- A checkers piece captures a chess piece by jumping it, and must keep jumping
  while it can.
- Checkers must capture when it can. Chess need not.
- A pawn reaching the far rank becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight; a man
  reaching it is crowned.

The chess king may never be left where a checkers piece could jump it - that
is check.

## How it ends

- Chess loses when it is checkmated, and draws when it is stalemated.
- Checkers loses when it has no move, whether it has pieces left or not.
- A draw after fifty moves each with no capture and no pawn or man moving, or
  when the same position comes round for the third time.
- Either player may resign at any time.

## Writing it down

Moves are written in algebraic notation, as in chess. A man has no letter,
like a pawn, and a crowned man is `C`. A chain of jumps lists every landing:
`c5xe3xg5`.
