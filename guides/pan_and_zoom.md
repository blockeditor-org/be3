Pan and zoom is a plugin capability (`capabilities.pan_and_zoom` in the manifest, see guides/adding_a_plugin_editor.md), not something an editor builds for itself. An editor with it declares that its content has a camera of its own - a world it draws at a position and a scale - and hands the camera to whatever is hosting it instead of keeping one. The point of that split is that the same editor code has to work whether it is sitting in a tab of its own or living inside another editor's canvas, without knowing which: "zoom in" means the same thing to the pixel art editor whether the person clicked its own toolbar button or turned a mouse wheel over it while it was a card on an infinite canvas, because in both cases the camera it draws through is something the host handed it, not something it owns.

## What the editor is given

The host hands the camera over as a rectangle and a scale, in the editor's own coordinates: `editor.canvas()` and `editor.scale()`, both signals the framework writes before every frame (`beui::reactive::CanvasView`, a plain `{origin, scale}`). The editor draws its content there and keeps no offset or zoom of its own - an instance never told where its view is just fills the space it has, at a scale of one, which is what happens the first frame and what happens to an editor with no camera to speak of. It moves the camera by asking rather than by touching its own state: `editor.pan(delta)`, `editor.zoom(factor)` or `editor.zoom_at(factor, anchor)`, which is what a `-` and `+` button call; `editor.reveal(rect)` pans a rectangle of the editor's own world into the middle of the content, which is what a jump-to-this list does.

The camera is the host's, and so is keeping it fitted; only what to fit is the editor's. `editor.fit(world, room, max_scale)` (`crates/block-editor-beui/src/fitting.rs`) is a move like any other: it asks for the zoom, around the centre of a rectangle of the editor's world, that makes it fill `room` (capped at `max_scale`), and a pan of that centre to the middle of `room`, the way the infinite canvas's Fit all and the logic grid's recenter use it (the canvas maps its own coordinates, centred on its origin or its frame, to the editor's world through `CanvasView::rect_to_canvas` first). An editor that should open showing its content, the way a picture or a page does, names its content band with `editor.content` and calls `editor.keep_fitted(|| Some(fit))` once, where `fit` is a `Fit`: `Fit::World` for one that lays its content out inside its world (the pixel art, pixel ray tracer, image, map and game editors), `Fit::Area(rect)` for a rectangle of its world (the page for the PDF editor, the cards' rectangle for `pan_zoom`), or `Fit::Centre`, the world's centre at a scale of one (the infinite canvas's origin). That sends the host `ViewChange::KeepFitted` with a `FitArea`, again only when it changes, and gets a `Fitting` whose `fit()` is its `Fit` button. The host does the fitting (`FitArea::fit` in block-plugin-api, at most a scale of one, which both the app's camera and block-ui-test's use) whenever it lays the frame out: the content band, the world or the chrome changing re-fits in the same frame, with nothing sent to the editor and back. That is why it is declared rather than sent as moves: a fit the editor worked out from the view it had been sent was stale whenever the frame resized over several frames, and the moves added up.

Whether it follows is the camera's alone: the host's camera (`Viewport` in `BlockFrameView`) is `moved` by every pan and zoom - a wheel, a pinch, a pan-drag, or an editor's own `pan`, `zoom` and `fit` (a `+` button, a jump to a card) - and stops following until `Fitting::fit()` sends `ViewChange::Fit`, which clears it and fits again. The camera keeps `moved` with the rest of its view, remembered per block for as long as the app runs, so a block reopened on a view the person moved opens where they left it rather than fitted, and one they never moved opens fitted. It does not go in the editor's view block: that outlives the camera it describes (an app restarted on a remembered `moved` would open on the default view, neither fitted nor theirs), and a tab opened again gets a new one. It follows only while the editor has a frame of its own and its chrome drawn: a card on a canvas, or a parent whose child has taken its frame over, leaves the camera where it is, and `fit()` there (no chrome, or a `placed_content`) is a single `editor.fit` through the camera the child is placed in, which a placed child's camera owner takes as a move by hand like any other. The infinite canvas's Fit all, which fits its first frame, or else everything on it, or else goes back to its origin, is an `editor.fit`, so a resize afterwards leaves it where Fit all put it.

Wheel, pinch, and a middle- or space-drag over the content belong to the host as well, read once for the outermost view under the pointer, so a wheel over a block being edited inside a canvas moves the canvas rather than both; which of them the host reads is the editor's `capabilities.pan`, so they are the same everywhere it is shown: `TwoFinger` (the default) is all of those, `None` is none of them, and `OneFinger` adds a plain drag or one finger. Only the editor can tell that drag from one it means for itself (moving a map point or a game piece), so the press goes to the editor, which calls `editor.pan_drag()` when it pressed on nothing of its own; the host cancels the pointer in the editor and moves the camera with it until it lifts, without asking the editor again on each move. The host takes them through `gesture_area` on its `Interactive`: beui gives the wheel, a pinch, a middle press and a press with space held to the outermost `Interactive` on the hit path whose `gesture_area` holds the point, ahead of the plugin region's input forwarder inside it, so the plugin never sees them. A trackpad's two-finger scroll is that wheel and its pinch that pinch on the web as natively (guides/beui.md, on platforms), and a pinch zooms around the pointer. `BlockFrameView` claims its content rectangle, less the floating chrome (a dock, a zoom pill) the editor reports. A finger on a rectangle the editor reports as `drawing` while canvas touch is off (guides/beui.md) pans and zooms nothing: the host's PluginRegion drops it before any of this.

Because the camera is something the editor is handed rather than something it keeps, it cannot tell a tab from a canvas by looking at it, and does not need to: `pan_zoom` (`crates/editors/pan-zoom`) is the reference implementation, and its whole view is built the same way regardless of where the host puts it.

## Who owns it, and where that lives

The host side of all this is `BlockFrameView` in `crates/block-app/src/compositor/editors.rs`, the component that shows one block's editor in a frame. Whether it owns a camera depends on how the block was placed:

- **A frame of its own** (a tab, or a child a parent places with its own chrome, through `TabFrame`): nothing above it has a camera for it, so it keeps one - a `Viewport` signal with a zoom and a pan offset, remembered per block so it survives the component being rebuilt. Wheel, pinch and pan-drag over the region, and the editor's own `ViewChange`s, settle into it, and the resulting content rectangle and scale go to the editor as its `EditorView` along with the region's placement.
- **Placed by a parent** (a card on a canvas, or a child that has taken the parent's frame over): the parent's camera already decides where the child lands, so the child keeps none. It is told the rectangle the parent placed it at, at the scale that rectangle has over the child's intrinsic size. Everything it asks of its view goes straight to the nearest frame above it that owns a camera (`CameraOwner`), never through the parent: a pan or zoom is applied to that camera as it is. A placed child without pan and zoom has no camera to hand a scale to, so the host scales it instead: it lays out at the size the parent assigned and is rendered at the rectangle's scale over that size (a larger scale factor, with input mapped back), the way a checklist card grows with the canvas. While a child has the frame, the parent's chrome is hidden, so a parent keeping itself fitted stops following, and hiding that chrome does not move the world under the child.

That is what makes the camera shared rather than merely handed down once: a zoom the focused child requests moves the camera the canvas is drawn through, which is why panning or zooming while focused into a card moves the canvas under it too.

A child that has taken the frame keeps the whole frame as its region while the camera moves, so a pan changes what it draws rather than where the host puts its texture, and it should change in the same frame as the canvas under it. The parent's answer saying where the child now sits comes back only after the parent has drawn, so the host does not wait for it: beui marks each `Embed` laid out inside a panning `Canvas` (`Document::panned_by_canvas`), the plugin reports those children as `follows_view`, and the host maps each such child's last rectangle from the view it had sent at the serial of the frame that placed it (guides/adding_a_plugin_editor.md, on drawing) to the one it is about to hand the parent (`view_rect` on `HostChild`). The child's view comes from that, so both editors are asked to draw for the new camera in the same frame. A card on a canvas does not need it: its view is its own rectangle, which a pan only moves, so the host moves its last texture with the parent's placements, which it takes in with the parent's frame.

A beui view that needs the same gestures without a host to hand it a camera -
a standalone canvas, or the demo's stage - composes `beui::unstyled::PanZoom`
instead, which reads wheel, pinch and middle-drag over its own rectangle and
reports a camera its caller owns. It is the gesture half of the same idea, not
a second camera: an editor placed on a host still takes the camera it is given
rather than keeping a `PanZoom` of its own.

## Drawing through it

An editor gets a `CanvasView` and hands it straight to a `<Canvas view={...}>` node (`crates/beui-view/src/components/canvas.rs`, over the node in `crates/beui-core/src/base/canvas.rs`). A `Canvas` does not lay its items out through the normal measure/layout pass at all - each `CanvasItem` child carries a rectangle in the editor's own world coordinates, and the canvas maps that rectangle through the view (`origin + point * scale`) to get where it paints on screen, deliberately ignoring the `Canvas` node's own laid-out rect. That is what lets an item's screen position track the shared camera exactly even though the surrounding layout (a sidebar taking width out of the frame, say) has no idea where the canvas sits on some ancestor's own canvas. Placing an item this way is not transforming it: text inside one keeps whatever size it asked for, so an interactive item whose content should grow with the zoom reads the scale and sizes its own text by it, the way `pan_zoom`'s cards scale their title and padding off the `scale` signal directly - and every such item is laid out and painted again on each frame of a zoom.

An item that only draws is transformed instead: `<CanvasItem scales=true>` is laid out at its rectangle in the canvas's own units, inside a space of the canvas (`Rects::set_scaled_space`, `Group::Scroll { scale, .. }` in the display list) that the view scales and translates, and its content draws in those units - the infinite canvas draws each entity at the shape's own size, never through the camera. A pan or a zoom then changes only that space's transform: no scaled item is laid out or painted again, the renderer scales the group (`beui-renderer-wgpu` multiplies its pixel factor), and damage is the canvas's viewport, derived from the group's identity. Text in a scaled item is laid out at the document's scale whatever the zoom, so it wraps in the same places at every zoom, and its glyphs are rasterised at the zoom it is shown at (guides/beui.md, layout and raster): the `Canvas` component holds that raster scale (`Document::set_canvas_raster`, `Zoom::raster`) while a zoom moves, so the frames of a zoom stretch the glyphs they have (a `Galley` records the pixel density its glyphs were rasterised at, `Galley::pixels_per_point`, and a flattened `Shape::Text` the scale its groups drew it at; `push_quads` sizes each glyph quad by the ratio of the density it is drawn at to the one it was rasterised at, which the wgpu renderer gets through its pixel factor), and sets it to the view's scale once the scale has stayed put for 150ms, which repaints the scaled items' text once at that raster without laying anything out. A scaled item takes pointer input like any other: beui hit-tests in window coordinates through each space's scale as well as its translation (`Rects::get`), and hands what it finds positions in its own units - a `PointerPress`, `ScrollGesture` or `ZoomGesture`'s `local` and `size`, a `HitRegion`, a `DragPoint` over a drop target - while `pos` stays in the window's. A finger dragging a scroll inside one moves it by the drag over the scale, so the content stays under the finger; a wheel's delta is passed on as it is. Text in one is edited in place: `TextGeometry` (`caret_rect`, `index_at`, `inline_at`, `bounds`) maps between the window and the text's layout through the scale, so a press or a drag places the caret and selects where it lands, the selection handles are hit where they are drawn (they grow with the zoom, as everything in the item does; `handle_center` and `toolbar_anchor` take the scale), and the IME area and the anchors of what opens from inside (the text menu at the pressed point, the completion menu under the caret, any menu or popover anchored to a node, `measure`) are window rectangles. `reveal` scrolls a scroll inside a scaled item by the extent over the scale, and the canvas pans its camera to show a focused item's caret. Only what has an `Interactive` takes a press: an item that only draws is transparent to the pointer, so presses fall through it to whatever is under it. An embedded child (a `ChildBlock`) still sits in an ordinary item.

An item with `pointer` false is drawn but lets every press through to whatever is under it, even its own `Interactive`s, which is how the infinite canvas only hands a text presses while it is being edited. The text stays in its scaled item while it is edited, a `styled::TextArea` laid out at the entity's own size and inset, so it wraps in the same places whether it is being edited or not; the text area reports its content size (`on_content_size`) in its own units, which the canvas grows the entity to.

A beui editor also reports back where its content ended up. It names the node its content is in once, with `editor.content(&node_ref)`, and the framework reads that node's rectangle after every layout - the host cannot see where a beui editor put its own sidebar, so this is how it learns what rectangle to actually pan, zoom, and read wheel gestures against, which is what keeps a wheel over the sidebar from being read as a wheel over the content.

## Why the frame is bigger than the content, and why that means staying transparent

A pan-and-zoom editor is handed the *whole* ancestor frame to draw in, not a rectangle clipped down to wherever it happens to sit on the parent canvas. That is deliberate: once you are focused into an editor you need somewhere for its real chrome to live - `pan_zoom`'s sidebar is a fixed 220px, not something that could fit inside a card's own footprint - and the content itself still belongs at the position and size the shared camera says, which can be smaller than, bigger than, or only partly overlapping that footprint once you start panning and zooming while focused.

Taking the frame over resizes the editor's screen from a card's footprint to the whole frame in one step, and a plugin draws a frame behind the host: the painting the host has in hand when that happens was made for the old size. Stretching it across the new one is a visible flash of the whole editor scaled up, so the host keeps the last painting at the rectangle it was made for until the editor has painted at the new size, and the same in reverse when the frame is handed back. Nothing about that reaches the editor - it is the host holding a frame, not the editor drawing one - and it applies only to the takeover. An ordinary resize is never stretched either: the painting stays at its own size from the region's top-left corner, cropped or short of the region at the right and bottom, with its children where it put them, until the editor paints at the new size (guides/adding_a_plugin_editor.md, on drawing).

That combination only looks right if the editor paints *just* its own content and chrome and leaves everything else alone. `crates/block-ui/src/frame.rs`'s `Frame` never fills the content band itself - it only ever creates a child `Ui` sized to the content rect and calls into the editor - and the plugin framework works the same way: "a frame is transparent wherever the editor does not paint, which is what lets the layer beneath show through around a block being edited inside a canvas" (guides/adding_a_plugin_editor.md). A `Canvas` node keeps to this by construction, since its `paint` only draws its items and nothing else. The mistake this guide exists to head off is wrapping the *whole* view in an opaque background the way a normal, non-canvas editor reasonably would for its one always-solid tab: `pan_zoom` used to wrap its entire view - sidebar, rail, and stage together - in a single `<Frame fill=ColorRole::Background>`, which looked correct as a standalone tab (there is nothing to show through to) and silently painted over the entire infinite canvas underneath it the moment it was focused as a child, because the stage now spanned the whole frame rather than a small footprint. The fix was to drop that outer background and let the sidebar and rail keep their own (they already had one each, since they need to stay legible over whatever is behind them), leaving the stage - which never had a background of its own beyond that wrapper - transparent everywhere it is not actively drawing a card. A canvas-style editor should follow the same shape: give fixed chrome its own opaque background where it genuinely needs one, and never wrap the pannable/zoomable content area itself in anything that paints its full bounds solid.
